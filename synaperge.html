Why it exists

The name joins synapse and emerge: connections between fields, and what comes out of them. The idea was simple. Most student discussions about technology end when the event does. Synaperge was meant to keep going—from a seminar, to a team working on a problem, to something shown to others for critique.

Seminars were deliberately not lectures. Members debated in real time and left with something to act on. That format, and the expectation that discussion should lead somewhere, shaped everything the organization later added.

“Innovation is never a solo act. At Synaperge, we believe progress emerges when disciplines converge, voices challenge one another, and ideas are built collaboratively—across boundaries, not within them.”

From my founder’s statement on the Synaperge website

From discussion to project

Each program had a place in a path that took a member from first contact to showing finished work. Not every member took every step; the structure made the next step visible.

Programs

Seminars

Sessions on AI ethics, innovation, and the social effects of technology; I curated seven of them. Discussion-led rather than lecture-led.

The Problem Lab

Interdisciplinary teams working on a defined real-world problem, which turned conversation into work with a deliverable.

Course: Introduction to Artificial Intelligence and Innovative Reasoning

A free Canvas course with a certificate of completion and no prerequisites in programming or mathematics. It covers AI fundamentals in plain terms, how innovation emerges across disciplines, and the ethical responsibilities behind intelligent systems. I led the planning and launch of the organization’s youth AI ethics course: its framing, session order, and learning outcomes.

Events and showcases

Virtual conferences and the Futurescape Showcase, where members presented work for critique, plus a summer 2024 retreat that brought 35 members together in person.

Synaperge and DFDraw Lab describe themselves as partner initiatives with overlapping leadership. Each operates independently.

Member projects I guided

Three community projects moved from early discussion to a defined plan under my guidance:

  1. Project 1

    AI bias in education technology

    How algorithmic tools used in schools can treat groups of students differently.

  2. Project 2

    The psychology of fast-food branding

    How brand design shapes choices, studied as a consumer-behaviour problem.

  3. Project 3

    Accessibility of financial apps

    Barriers in the user experience of personal-finance software.

What I did

  • Built the membership model. Recruitment, onboarding, and engagement routines that connected members across disciplines and countries.
  • Designed and moderated seminars on AI ethics, innovation strategy, and social impact.
  • Planned the youth AI ethics course, including how it was framed, the order of sessions, and what learners should be able to do by the end.
  • Guided three member projects from exploratory idea to an actionable plan.
  • Distributed leadership. Mentoring, delegation, and a rotating board so that initiatives had owners other than me—the condition for the organization outlasting any one person’s involvement.
