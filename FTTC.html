The role

I provide front-line support and resolve problems that cross several layers at once: endpoint computers, networks and connectivity, peripherals, accounts, and services specific to Anderson. Much of the job is translation. The person asking for help usually describes a symptom—“Zoom can’t see my camera,” “I can’t get in”—and the cause is often one or two layers away from where it shows up.

A resolved problem is only half the result. The other half is that the person understands what happened well enough not to be stuck the same way again, and leaves less wary of the technology than when they arrived.

Where problems live

A useful first question is which layer a problem belongs to. Symptoms surface at the top; causes can sit anywhere below. Select a layer.

What the work takes

Front-line support

Meeting people where they are, clarifying the problem, and turning a technical issue into a clear next step.

Account solutions

Resolving access, authentication, and service questions with attention to security and continuity.

Endpoint readiness

Working across hardware, software, peripherals, and connectivity so people can get back to class, research, or work.

Translation

Explaining options in plain language to people who did not come to learn about technology and should not have to.

The same habit runs through my research: a visible problem is usually the top layer of a stack, and the fix depends on finding the right layer. See the Housing Friction Index.
