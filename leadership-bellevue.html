The plan’s framework

The district organizes its plan around three commitments—Belong, Learn, Lead—resting on a foundation of sustainable systems: the financial, operational, and equity conditions that make the other three possible.

How input became a plan

  1. Coalition

    Students, caregivers, educators, and community leaders discuss priorities and barriers.

  2. Goals and measures

    Priorities become goals, each with ways of measuring progress.

  3. Annual plans

    Each year’s plan turns the five-year commitments into concrete steps.

  4. Feedback

    Results and community feedback adjust the next year’s plan.

My contribution

I was one voice among many. My input concentrated on three things:

  • Qualitative measures alongside numbers. Where goals were set as quantitative targets, I argued for measures that capture what the numbers miss—belonging, participation, access, and the barriers students themselves report.
  • Student voice. Advising district leaders on policies that give students a larger and more direct role in decisions that affect them.
  • Equity within constraints. Contributing equity-focused input to the five-year strategies, which had to be balanced against a district budget deficit of roughly $20 million.

The coalition’s approach—diagnose with the people affected first, then design measurable interventions—is close to how I approach research. It was useful to see it applied at the scale of a public institution, with real budgets and competing priorities.
