02In detail

What each method showed

Select a method.

Household finance paper

Survey design and sampling

A 28-item questionnaire in four domains (demographics, current tools, limitations, desired features) with 5-point Likert satisfaction items, piloted and revised for scale format and item order, then fielded for three weeks. Voluntary non-probability sampling with three inclusion criteria; 342 responses, 331 after cleaning (96.8%).

Limit: a voluntary online sample skews toward digitally literate users, so results describe that population rather than all households.

Household finance paper · Housing Friction Index

Descriptive statistics

Finance: mean satisfaction 2.7 of 5 (SD 1.1), lowest for freelancers (2.3) and co-managing couples (2.4); 64.3% use two or more tools and 34.5% three or more. Housing: mean concern intensity across seven interviews, from 4.9 for mortgage rates to 3.1 for the local tax; 101 coded open-house topic mentions led by monthly payment (26) and rates (23).

Limit: the housing figures come from small, deliberately local samples and describe patterns, not populations.

Household finance paper

One-way ANOVA

Tested whether satisfaction with current finance tools differs across income groups. It does: F(2, 328) = 5.84, p < .01. Combined with the group means, the reading is that volatile income makes inadequate tools feel worse.

Limit: ANOVA shows that group means differ, not which pairs differ or why; group sizes were not reported.

Household finance paper · Housing Friction Index

Correlation

Finance: the number of tools used correlates with reported cognitive fatigue, r = 0.58, p < .001—the study’s central quantitative support for the fragmentation hypothesis. Housing: a modeled association of 0.39 between REET exposure and days on market, used only to decide what to test next.

Limit: correlation is not causation; the housing value is not an observed estimate at all, which is why the project specifies a control-group design.

Household finance paper

Exploratory factor analysis

Fourteen pain-point items, principal axis factoring with Promax rotation. Sampling adequacy KMO = 0.84; Bartlett’s test χ²(91) = 973.24, p < .001. Three factors explained 71.2% of variance: integration friction, forecasting insufficiency, and collaboration difficulty—which became the three design priorities.

Limit: an exploratory structure from one sample; confirming it would need a confirmatory analysis on new data.

Household finance paper

Usability metrics

Sixteen participants, five counterbalanced tasks. Task completion 94%; mean time on task 3.4 minutes (SD 1.1); 0.47 errors per task, mostly in permission settings; System Usability Scale 84.2 (SD 7.9).

Limit: a remote session with simulated data measures usability, not whether the tool changes financial behaviour over time.

All three projects

Qualitative coding

Finance: 35 semi-structured interviews and screen-shared task observation, synthesized into requirements. Housing: seven practitioner interviews coded by topic, concern intensity (1–5), and agreement level; open-house conversations coded once per visitor group. BSD: qualitative and experiential measures designed to sit alongside every numerical target.

Housing Friction Index

Composite index construction

Six components normalized to a 0–100 scale and weighted: REET burden 25%, zoning 20%, parking 15%, permit delay 15%, mortgage-rate exposure 15%, street-width walkability 10%. Interpretation bands: above 70, several channels active; 40–70, mixed; below 40, lower exposure.

Limit: the weights are stated assumptions, published so they can be challenged; the index is for comparison, not forecasting.

Housing Friction Index

Cost and scenario modeling

Transaction-cost arithmetic across sale-price bands: Washington’s graduated state excise tax plus Bellevue’s 0.5% local rate, compared with a no-local-tax scenario. At $1.4 million the local share is $7,000. Try the calculator.

Housing Friction Index

Causal research design

To test whether the local tax itself lengthens listings, the study specifies a matched comparison: Bellevue against a high-income ZIP code in a municipality without local REET, same months, controlling for property type, price band, rate period, and season, with days on market, price reductions, and list-to-sale ratio as outcomes.

Status: designed, not yet run; until then, results are exploratory.

Household finance · Housing · BSD

Business analytics

Finance: feature ratings (simulation 4.64, permissions 4.42, dashboard 4.37) set build priorities, and the product concept was paired with a business model of partner and affiliate revenue. Housing: municipal revenue purpose weighed against market effects, with policy alternatives—thresholds, exemptions, credits—framed as trade-offs. BSD: goals tied to budget decisions, including a fund-balance target from −$5 million to at least −$3 million.

BSD goals and measures · Household finance

Measurement design

Choosing what to measure before measuring it. The district plan pairs every quantitative target with qualitative and experiential evidence and disaggregates results by student group. The finance survey was built in four domains so that satisfaction, behaviour, and unmet needs could be analysed separately.

03Practice

How I report numbers

  1. State the scale

    2.7 out of 5 and 84.2 out of 100 come from different instruments. I never put them on one axis.

  2. Separate kinds of evidence

    Observed, coded, modeled, and assumed figures are labeled differently on every chart.

  3. Show the spread

    Means are shown with standard deviations where the source reports them.

  4. Name the limit

    Every project page ends with what the analysis cannot show.
