01Profile

Practical problems, examined as systems

Most of my work starts with a practical problem that turns out to have several layers underneath it.

A family moving money between five apps. A 0.5% tax line on a closing statement. A laptop that will not join the network an hour before class. In each case the visible issue sits on top of interacting parts—software, policy, habits, institutions. I use statistics, interviews, and close observation to find those parts, then build something people can use: an interface, a framework, a support routine, an organization.

Alongside that, I write about what happens to human judgment when software suggests, ranks, and decides: two books so far, and an ongoing series of essays.

02Selected work

Research and projects

The two projects with the most evidence behind them, then two organizations I have helped run.

Peer-reviewed paper · Human–computer interaction

Revolutionizing Household Finance: A User-Centered Platform for Holistic Financial Management

Dang, H. & Ding, F. · HCI International 2025 – Late Breaking Papers, LNCS 16342, pp. 22–40 · Springer, 2026

Households track budgets, bills, accounts, and forecasts in separate apps. We surveyed 342 people and interviewed 35, found low satisfaction with existing tools, and iterated a single platform with shared family planning. In usability testing the prototype received a mean satisfaction score of 84.2 out of 100.

Independent research · Housing policy · Jan–Jun 2026

Housing Friction Index

Bellevue, Washington · policy review, seven practitioner interviews, open-house fieldwork

Bellevue’s 0.5% local real estate excise tax adds $7,000 to a $1.4 million sale. I used that line item as an entry point to model how taxes, zoning, parking, permitting, mortgage rates, and supply accumulate into affordability pressure—and found that, on its own, the tax is one of the smaller forces.
